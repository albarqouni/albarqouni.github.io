abstract: Chest X-rays are an important diagnostic tool, but their interpretation by radiologists can be complex and time consuming. Deep Learning (DL) was identified as a possible solution to this problem, but it requires large and well-annotated datasets. This requirement represents a significant limitation due to the difficulties in accessing patient data and ensuring their confidentiality. Federated Learning (FL) emerged as a promising alternative, as it allows hospitals to share local models while maintaining patient data within the hospital. However, the decisions made by the FL models are difficult to interpret and prevent radiologists from validating the results. In this paper we propose a methodology that adapts a lightweight version of the ConvNeXt architecture for automatic diagnosis of chest radiographs within an FL framework, evaluating the impact of adversarial attacks on model training.
